What we file

Micro Company Accounts Go to Companies House and the Tax Return Goes to HMRC

Filing micro company accounts splits into two submissions with two different contents. Companies House sees a reduced public record. HMRC sees the full financial picture through the Company Tax Return.

Companies House receives the balance sheet and little else. We prepare micro-entity accounts under FRS 105, which means a cover page carrying your company name and registration number, a simplified balance sheet for a director to sign, and four mandatory footnotes: employee numbers, called-up share capital not paid, off-balance sheet arrangements where any exist, and a statement that the accounts sit under the micro-entities regime. Your turnover and profit stay off the public record.

HMRC receives the full picture. We send the same balance sheet plus the CT600 form carrying turnover, expenses and taxable profit. HMRC accepts iXBRL accounts with the balance sheet alone because the CT600 supplies the figures the tax calculation needs.

A Company Tax Return is three documents, not one. A CT600 filing consists of the CT600 form itself, statutory accounts in iXBRL format, and a Corporation Tax computation, all filed together. We prepare all three from your records.

Qualification

Does Your Company Qualify to File Micro Company Accounts?

A company qualifies to file micro company accounts when it meets the micro-entity size test and sits outside the excluded categories. Four checks settle the answer, and your accountant works through each one before we prepare your filing.

Filing Micro Company Accounts Against the Three Size Thresholds

The micro-entity test measures size on three axes and requires only two of them. Your company is a micro-entity if it has any 2 of the following: a turnover of £1,000,000 or less, £500,000 or less on its balance sheet, and 10 employees or less. Meeting all three is not necessary.

The same test reduces to three questions a director can answer in seconds. Does turnover sit under £1,000,000? Does the balance sheet sit under £500,000? Does the company employ fewer than 10 people? A "yes" to 2 or more means you should get in touch.

The thresholds moved recently. For periods starting on or after 6 April 2025 the government increased them from the previous £632,000 turnover and £316,000 balance sheet limits. A company that failed the old test may therefore pass the current one.

A worked example makes the test concrete. A consultancy turns over £180,000, holds £42,000 on its balance sheet, and employs 2 people. It meets all three thresholds, so it qualifies as a micro-entity comfortably, and it needed only 2 of the 3 to do so.

Filing Micro Company Accounts in the First Year and in Later Years

The qualification rule changes after the first year. A company generally qualifies as a micro-entity in its first financial year if it fulfils the conditions in that year. In any subsequent year, the company must fulfil the conditions in that year and in the year before, so the test looks back across two consecutive years.

A single bad year does not end the exemption. Where a company qualified as a micro-entity in one year and no longer meets the criteria in the next, it may continue to claim the exemptions available in that next year.

Recovery is seamless. If the company then reverts to being a micro-entity by meeting the criteria in the following year, the exemption continues uninterrupted. Your accountant checks the prior year figures before applying the regime.

This distinction matters to the fee. Qualification for the regime and the scope of our fee are two different tests. Our £445 fee covers total income up to £40,000 and total assets up to £90,000, which sit well below the statutory micro-entity thresholds.

Companies That Cannot File Micro Company Accounts

Some companies fall outside the micro-entity regime whatever their size. Your company cannot file micro-entity accounts if it is, or was at any point during the financial year, a limited or qualifying partnership, a public limited company, an overseas company, an unregistered company, a company authorised to register under the Companies Act, or a charitable company.

The list closes with companies sitting outside the small company regime altogether, along with investment companies, financial institutions such as insurers and banks, and companies forming part of an ineligible group. All are excluded on the same footing.

We check every one of these before committing your company to the regime. Anything caught by an exclusion files small company accounts instead, and we quote that work against your figures rather than applying the fee on this page.

One risk deserves attention here. A company that files micro-entity accounts while excluded from the regime files the wrong accounts. That error can expose the company to a rejected filing, and to the late filing penalties that follow if the corrected accounts miss the deadline.

Choosing Between Micro Company Accounts and Small Company Accounts

The small company regime sits one step above the micro-entity regime. A company qualifies as small with any 2 of the following: a turnover of £15 million or less, £7.5 million or less on its balance sheet, and 50 employees or less. Small companies can use audit exemptions and can submit abridged accounts to Companies House.

A qualifying micro-entity can still choose the small company route on purpose. Four reasons commonly drive that choice: you need to show creditors more detailed financial information, your group requires FRS 102 for consolidation, you want to disclose profit and loss publicly, or your company will exceed the micro-entity thresholds next year.

We prepare accounts under either regime. Where your figures sit outside the scope of this page, we quote for the work against your income and assets rather than applying a fixed fee.

Your company may have had no trading at all. A company with no significant accounting transaction files dormant company accounts and a company tax return instead, which we price as a separate service.

Corporation Tax

Micro Company Accounts Feed the Corporation Tax Your Company Pays

The accounts and the tax bill are connected. Corporation Tax is payable annually and is based on the profit that your company makes. That profit includes a deduction for salaries paid, and it excludes dividends paid.

The rate depends on the size of the profit. Current Corporation Tax rates are 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% above £250,000, with marginal relief tapering the rate for profits in between. A micro company within this page's scope, with total income up to £40,000, therefore sits in the 19% band in the ordinary case.

The computation is not the same as the accounting profit. We build it by starting from accounting profit, adding back disallowable costs such as depreciation and entertainment, deducting capital allowances, and applying any losses to reach taxable profit.

Here is that computation worked through. A company with £68,000 accounting profit, £4,000 added back for depreciation, and £6,000 deducted in capital allowances arrives at £66,000 taxable profit. That figure sits between £50,000 and £250,000, so marginal relief tapers the rate down from the 25% main rate to an effective rate in the low 20s.

Filing is required even at a loss. Your company must file a Company Tax Return even if you make a loss or have no Corporation Tax to pay, and reported losses carry forward against future profits only where HMRC holds a record of them.

Personal tax follows the company tax. Salaries and dividends drawn from your company are subject to Income Tax, and your salary should be paid net of Income Tax through PAYE unless you meet certain criteria not to register for PAYE, in which case Income Tax is paid through Self Assessment. Our fee includes ongoing support on VAT, PAYE and Income Tax, so the same adviser handles those questions.

Deadlines and penalties

Late Micro Company Accounts Expose the Company to Penalties on Two Fronts

Companies House and HMRC each run a separate penalty regime, and both regimes apply whether or not the company owes tax. A company that files on time reduces its exposure under both to zero.

The Companies House deadline runs from the accounting reference date. Unless you are filing your company's first accounts, the time normally allowed for delivering accounts to Companies House is 9 months from the accounting reference date for a private company. First accounts covering a period of more than 12 months must arrive within 21 months of the date of incorporation, or 3 months from the accounting reference date, whichever is longer.

Failure to deliver accounts on time is a criminal offence. The law additionally imposes a penalty for late filing of accounts on the company, and the amount of that penalty depends on how late the accounts arrive.

Micro Company Accounts and Company Tax Return: Common Questions

Directors ask the same six clusters of questions before they file micro company accounts. Each tab below answers one cluster.

Who files the micro company accounts, you or the director?

We file them. We send the accounts and tax return to you for your approval, explain what tax is owed, answer your questions, and then file directly with HMRC and Companies House.

Does filing with HMRC also cover Companies House?

No, and treating it that way is one of the most common mistakes. Both bodies require accounts, and they require them on different deadlines. We file with both as part of the fee.

What must appear on the balance sheet itself?

The balance sheet must carry a statement in a prominent position above the director's signature and printed name, confirming that the accounts have been prepared in accordance with the micro-entity provisions. That statement must appear in the original accounts as well as in the copy sent to Companies House.

What if the company used accounting software, or used none at all?

Either position works. We integrate with a range of accounting software packages, and if you use none of them that is also fine.

What is a micro-entity?

Micro-entities are very small companies. Your company is a micro-entity if it has any 2 of the following: a turnover of £1,000,000 or less, £500,000 or less on its balance sheet, and 10 employees or less.

Do I qualify as a micro-entity every year?

A company generally qualifies in its first financial year if it fulfils the conditions in that year. In subsequent years it must fulfil the conditions in that year and the year before. A company that stops meeting the criteria may continue to claim the exemptions in the next year, and the exemption continues uninterrupted if it reverts the year after.

Which entities cannot prepare micro-entity accounts?

A limited or qualifying partnership, a public limited company, an overseas company, an unregistered company, a company authorised to register under the Companies Act, and a charitable company. Companies excluded from the small company regime, investment companies, banks and insurers, and members of an ineligible group are outside it too. We check all of these before we file.

Did the size thresholds change recently?

When must micro company accounts reach Companies House?

Companies House normally allows 9 months from the accounting reference date for a private company. As a worked date, a company whose accounting period ends 31 January 2025 must file with Companies House by 31 October 2025.

When is the Company Tax Return due?

The deadline for the tax return is 12 months after the end of the accounting period it covers. A separate deadline applies to paying the Corporation Tax bill, usually 9 months and one day after the end of the accounting period.

What do Companies House and HMRC charge for late filing?

Companies House charges private companies £150, £375, £750 and £1,500 by band, doubled across 2 successive late years. HMRC charges £200 after 1 day, a further £200 at 3 months, 10% of unpaid tax at 6 months, and a further 10% at 12 months, rising to £1,000 for a third consecutive late return.

Can a Company Tax Return be amended after filing?

Yes, within 12 months of the filing deadline, usually by resubmitting the corrected return through the filing software. Overpaid tax can often still be reclaimed through an overpayment relief claim after that window closes.

Do I need to pay Corporation Tax?

Corporation Tax is payable annually and is based on the profit that your company makes. That profit includes a deduction for salaries paid, and it excludes dividends paid.

Does a loss-making company still file?

Yes. Your company must file a Company Tax Return even if you make a loss or have no Corporation Tax to pay. Reported losses carry forward against future profits only where HMRC holds a record of them.

What are the current Corporation Tax rates?

Corporation Tax runs at 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% above £250,000, with marginal relief tapering the rate for profits in between.

Do I also pay Income Tax and National Insurance?

Salaries and dividends drawn from your company are subject to Income Tax. Your salary should be paid net of Income Tax through PAYE unless you meet certain criteria not to register for PAYE, in which case Income Tax is paid through Self Assessment.

What is FRS 105?

FRS 105 is the micro-entities regime. Under it you file only a balance sheet with Companies House, and no profit and loss account is required publicly.

Which footnotes are mandatory?

Four: the number of employees, called-up share capital not paid, off-balance sheet arrangements where any exist, and a statement that the accounts are prepared under the micro-entities regime.

What changes in April 2028?

From April 2028 micro-entities must file profit and loss accounts with Companies House, though a company can opt out of publishing the profit and loss on the public register. Small companies cannot file abridged accounts under the new rules.

Can a director still file a CT600 through HMRC's free service?

No. HMRC closed its free Company Accounts and Tax Online filing service on 31 March 2026. From 1 April 2026 you need commercial software, or an accountant who files on your behalf.
