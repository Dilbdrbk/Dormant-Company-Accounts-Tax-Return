Know the basics

What Qualifies as a Micro-Entity?

Your company is a micro-entity if it meets any two of these three conditions. It does not need to meet all three.

Turnover of £1 million or less

£500,000 or less on the balance sheet

10 employees or less

How the two-year rule works

In your first financial year, meeting the limits in that year is enough. After that, qualification runs on a two-year basis: gaining or losing micro-entity status only counts if it happens in two consecutive financial years. One unusually good year does not push you out of the regime.

The higher thresholds apply to financial years beginning on or after 6 April 2025, and the transitional rules let you apply them to the previous period as well. Companies that failed the old £632,000 turnover test may qualify now without having changed anything.

Short and long accounting periods

The turnover limit is adjusted in proportion for any period that isn't a full year. A nine-month period gives £750,000, not £1 million. This catches first-year companies most often, since a first accounting period is rarely exactly twelve months. The employee figure is a monthly average across the year, not a headcount on your year-end date.

Prepared, filed, and published are three different things

FRS 105 requires a profit and loss account to be prepared. Only your balance sheet currently goes on the public register at Companies House. HMRC receives the full figures with your CT600 regardless. That gap closes in April 2028, covered below.

Size isn't the only test. A company excluded from the small companies regime can't be a micro-entity either, and a parent company that prepares group accounts can't qualify at all. Charities and regulated financial companies sit outside the regime. Qualifying is also optional: you can file small company accounts instead if you'd rather show more.

Filing Deadlines

  • Annual accounts9 months after your accounting reference date
  • First accounts21 months after incorporation, or 3 months after the ARD, whichever is longer
  • Corporation Tax payment9 months and 1 day after your period ends
  • CT600 tax return12 months after your period ends
  • Confirmation statementWithin 14 days of your review period ending

Your tax is due three months before your tax return is. That ordering catches people out every year.

Late Filing Penalties

  • Up to 1 month late£150
  • 1–3 months late£375
  • 3–6 months late£750
  • Over 6 months late£1,500

Those are Companies House penalties for late accounts, and they double if you file late two years running. HMRC charges separately for a late CT600: £200 at one day, another £200 at three months, then 10% of unpaid tax at six months and another 10% at twelve. Three late returns in a row raise the £200 charges to £1,000 each. Both regulators can penalise the same year-end.

Coming change

How Micro Company Accounts Change in April 2028

Companies House confirmed this on 9 June 2026. Most guidance still online gives the date as April 2027, or says the reforms were paused with no date at all. Both are out of date.

Five things change for a micro-entity from April 2028:

  • You'll file a profit and loss account with Companies House, with the option to opt out of publishing it on the public register. The opt-out is new, and the mechanism hasn't been published yet.
  • All accounts must be filed through commercial software in iXBRL format. This applies whether you file yourself or use an accountant.
  • Companies House web and paper filing close for accounts. Web filing stays open for confirmation statements and director updates.
  • Claiming audit exemption will need a strengthened eligibility statement on the balance sheet.
  • Shortening your accounting reference period gets limited, which closes a route some companies use to buy extra filing time.
Nothing needs doing before then, and every company gets one full accounting year plus nine months to prepare. Micro Filer already files through commercial software, so clients see no change.

Abridged accounts also disappear in the same package. That one affects small companies rather than micro-entities, since the micro-entity regime never used them.

Common Questions

Do micro-entities need an audit?+

No, in almost every case. A micro-entity sits well inside the small company size test, and small companies can take the audit exemption. Two things override it. Your articles of association can require an audit, and shareholders holding at least 10% of shares, by number or value, can demand one in writing, sent to your registered office at least a month before your financial year ends. Either way, an audit exemption statement has to appear on your balance sheet.

Do micro-entity accounts have to include a profit and loss account?+

Yes to prepare, no to publish, and the difference matters. FRS 105 requires a profit and loss account to be prepared. Only your balance sheet goes on the public register at Companies House today. HMRC gets the full figures with your CT600 either way. From April 2028 the profit and loss account gets filed with Companies House too, with an option to keep it off the public register.

Can a micro-entity file small company accounts instead?+

Yes. The regime is a choice, not an obligation. Some companies file fuller accounts deliberately, usually when a lender, an investor or a potential buyer will be reading them. FRS 105 also can't recognise defined benefit pension balances, so a company with that kind of scheme is normally better off under FRS 102 Section 1A.

What happens if my company grows past the thresholds?+

Nothing immediately. You lose micro-entity status only after exceeding the limits in two consecutive financial years, and the same two-year rule applies on the way in. If your company settles above the micro-entity size, Small Company Accounts is the right filing.

Do I still file with Companies House if I owe no Corporation Tax?+

Yes. Companies House and HMRC are separate regulators with separate obligations. A loss-making year still needs accounts filed with Companies House and a CT600 filed with HMRC. The £200 late filing penalty from HMRC applies even when there's no tax to pay, because it's charged for the late return rather than the unpaid tax.

How long does filing take?+

Most accounts and returns are finished 2–7 days after your records reach us. A 7-day turnaround is available on request if you're close to a deadline.
