Know the basics

What Qualifies as a Dormant Company?

A company is dormant under the Companies Act 2006 if it has had no significant accounting transaction during the financial year. Three things don't count against you:

  1. Companies House filing fees
  2. Late filing penalties
  3. The original payment for subscriber shares at incorporation

Almost everything else does count, including buying or selling anything, renting property, advertising, employing someone, or earning bank interest. HMRC and Companies House run two separate dormancy tests, and a company can meet one without meeting the other.

A dormant company with a bank account earning interest can be dormant for Companies House and not dormant for HMRC at the same time.

If HMRC decides to treat your company as dormant and confirms this in writing, no Company Tax Return is needed while that status holds. If you're notifying HMRC yourself and you've previously filed a return or received a notice to deliver one, you'll need to file one more Company Tax Return to formally close that period. (Full detail in the FAQ below.)

Companies that have carried on no trade or business at all during the period are usually excluded from the "associated companies" Corporation Tax count, so a genuinely dormant holding company doesn't normally reduce a sister company's tax-rate thresholds. The trap: this is HMRC's stricter test, not Companies House's. A company can file dormant accounts and still fail this exclusion if it had any activity at all, a single invoice, rental income, or bank interest are enough.

Filing Deadlines

  • Dormant accounts9 months after your accounting reference date
  • First accounts21 months after incorporation, or 3 months after the ARD, whichever is longer
  • Confirmation statementWithin 14 days of your review period ending, every year
  • Restarting tradingNotify HMRC within 3 months

Late Filing Penalties

  • Up to 1 month late£150
  • 1–3 months late£375
  • 3–6 months late£750
  • Over 6 months late£1,500

These double if you file late in two years running. A late confirmation statement works differently: no scaled fee, but Companies House can fine you up to £5,000 and strike your company off the register.

Common Questions

Do dormant companies need to file a tax return?+

Not always. If HMRC has decided to treat your company as dormant and confirms this in writing, you don't pay Corporation Tax or file a Company Tax Return while that status continues. If you're the one telling HMRC your company has gone dormant, and you'd previously filed a return or received a notice to deliver one, you'll need to file one final Company Tax Return to formally show HMRC the company is now dormant. After that, no further returns are needed unless HMRC sends a new notice.

Do I still need to file with Companies House if I'm dormant for HMRC?+

Yes. Companies House and HMRC are separate regulators with separate requirements. Dormant company accounts and a confirmation statement go to Companies House every year, regardless of your HMRC status.

What happens if I restart trading?+

Tell HMRC within 3 months of trading starting again. You don't need to tell Companies House separately; your next set of non-dormant accounts will show the change.

Can a dormant company have a bank account?+

Yes, but earning interest on it can affect your dormant status for HMRC, even if Companies House still treats you as dormant.
